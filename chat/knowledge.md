# ATL Access assistant knowledge

## About this site
ATL Access helps older adults and people with disabilities find accessible places in metro Atlanta and pick a good hour to go. You choose what helps you get around (wheelchair or scooter, cannot walk far, low vision or blind, deaf or hard of hearing, plain words), a type of place (pharmacy, hospital, clinic, library, senior center, community center, grocery, restroom), and a starting point. The site ranks places and shows how hot, cold or dark it will be at the time you choose.

## How access labels work
Each place shows one of four labels. Step-free access reported means volunteers on OpenStreetMap recorded the place as wheelchair accessible. Partly accessible means limited access. Reported not accessible means it was recorded as not accessible. Access not reported means nobody has recorded it, which is not the same as inaccessible. Always call ahead to confirm, because access changes and elevators break.

## MARTA rail and bus
The site uses MARTA's public schedule data. It lists MARTA rail stations and bus stops and whether MARTA marks each stop as wheelchair accessible. Before you leave, check that the elevator at your station is working. If you cannot use regular buses or trains, MARTA Mobility gives door-to-door rides after you apply. The current phone numbers and the application are on MARTA's official page: https://itsmarta.com/marta-mobility.aspx.
Under the Americans with Disabilities Act, transit agencies with regular bus or rail routes must also offer complementary paratransit for people whose disability prevents them from using those routes, within a service area near the routes.

## Heat safety
On hot days go in the morning, drink water before you feel thirsty, rest in an air-conditioned place such as a library, store or senior center, and keep outdoor walks short. Older adults, people who take certain medicines, and people with heart or lung conditions are at higher risk. Warning signs of heat illness include confusion, fainting, hot dry skin or heavy sweating with weakness, nausea, and a very high body temperature. Call 911 for these signs.
The site's "feels like" numbers are historical averages for the chosen month and hour, not a forecast.

## Cold and ice
In freezing weather watch for ice on ramps, curbs and sidewalks, wear layers and gloves, and keep your phone warm. Cold can make joints stiffer and mobility devices harder to control. Dial 211 to ask about warming centers.

## After dark
After dark prefer well lit, busy routes and consider a ride. The site's dial shows daylight for the chosen month. Tell someone your plan and keep your phone charged.

## Tips for wheelchair and scooter users
Ask whether there is a step-free entrance, a working elevator, wide doors and an accessible restroom before you go. Check the ramp and curb cuts on the way. Allow extra time for elevators at stations. If a stop is marked not accessible or unknown, pick another stop or call MARTA.

## Tips for blind and low vision visitors
The site has a mouse reader that speaks whatever the pointer is over, a read aloud button on each place, large text, and a high contrast mode. Ask a staff member to meet you at the entrance, and confirm whether tactile paving or audible signals exist. All results are also in a plain list, so you do not need the map.

## Tips for deaf and hard of hearing visitors
Ask for written communication, bring a note with your request, or use a text relay or video relay service. Some places have hearing loops, and the site marks the ones that volunteers recorded.

## Plain words mode
Choose "Plain, simple words" to get shorter lists and shorter answers.

## Help numbers
911 for emergencies. 988 for the suicide and crisis lifeline, call or text. 211 (United Way) for rides, food, cooling and warming centers and senior services. 311 for City of Atlanta issues such as broken sidewalks and curb ramps. For MARTA Mobility use the official MARTA page.

## Limits
This is a student project for the TSA AI Hackathon. It is not medical, legal or emergency advice. Hours, access and elevators change, so call to confirm. Place data comes from OpenStreetMap volunteers and MARTA, so it can be out of date or incomplete.
